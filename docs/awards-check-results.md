Why the removes:
- BAFTA's film documentary award ran 1948 to 1990 and was reintroduced in 2012 (History section of https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BAFTA_Award_for_Best_Documentary), so no documentary-category BAFTA exists for 1991 to 2011.
- Man on Wire: its February 2009 BAFTA was Outstanding British Film (62nd BAFTA).
- The Imposter: won Outstanding Debut by a British Writer, Director or Producer (66th BAFTA, 2013); in Best Documentary 2013 it was only a nominee (Searching for Sugar Man won).
- Touching the Void: won the Alexander Korda Award for Outstanding British Film (57th BAFTA, 2004).
- The Power of Nightmares: BAFTA TV award, Best Factual Series 2005.
- Weird Weekends: Louis Theroux's 2001 BAFTA TV awards (Richard Dimbleby Award, Best Presenter) are TV/personal awards.
- Judgement call: if the club wants BAFTA TV documentary awards counted, the last two could instead be fixed to dated TV labels, but the brief's table says documentary category only, so I've recommended removal.

No missing Best Documentary Feature wins or nominations were found: every catalogue film on the Oscar list already carries the label, with the right year. All Cannes labels check out, and no other catalogue film has won the Palme d'Or or L'Œil d'or.

Note: Waltz with Bashir's 2009 nomination was in the category then called Best Foreign Language Film (renamed Best International Feature in 2019). The existing label is fine with the current name.

## Notes on years and judgement calls

- DGA, PGA, Critics' Choice Movie Awards and Peabody: the source lists the film or programme year. Each label above uses the ceremony year, which is the list year plus one. For example, the 78th DGA Awards honoured 2025 films and were held in 2026. Peabody's own site labels winners by programme year, so if the site prefers that, subtract one from every Peabody line.
- Critics' Choice: the Movie Awards' documentary category ran from 1996 to 2016 (ceremonies). The separate Critics' Choice Documentary Awards ceremony started in Nov 2016, so O.J.: Made in America (Nov 2016) and Amy (Jan 2016) don't clash. In 2024 there was a tie with Will & Harper, which is not in the catalogue.
- Cinema Eye: the Wikipedia overview page has the wrong edition numbers from 2021 on. I used each edition's own page: the 15th was held 1 Mar 2022 (Flee) and the 18th on 9 Jan 2025 (No Other Land).
- CPH:DOX 2004: Darwin's Nightmare shared the award with three other films.
- Sheffield: I counted the Grand Jury Award only. Before 2014 the top jury prize was called "Special Jury Prize" (The Act of Killing 2013, The Interrupters 2011). I left those out, but they could be added as "Sheffield DocFest Special Jury Prize".
- IDFA: the Joris Ivens / Best Feature-Length award ran until 2020. From 2021 the prize is "Best Film, International Competition", and none of its winners are in the catalogue.
- Grierson: "Best Documentary" is not a single category. I used Best Cinema Documentary as the main line (plus My Architect 2005, which won Best International Cinema Documentary). Wins in other categories are in the OPTIONAL section.
- Peabody "freedom-riders 2012" was given to American Experience for three films, Freedom Riders among them. "up-series 2013" is an institutional award to Michael Apted's Up series. Treat both as borderline.
- Not checked: Venice prizes other than the Golden Lion, and Peabody before 1990.

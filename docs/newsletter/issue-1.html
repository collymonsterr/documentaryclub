Pick of the week · Editor's Choice

AlphaGo

2017 · Greg Kohs · 90 min · IMDb 7.8 · Rotten Tomatoes 100%

In March 2016, Google's DeepMind sat its AI down opposite Lee Sedol, one of the greatest Go players alive, for a five-game match in Seoul with a million dollars on the line. Go was meant to be the game computers couldn't crack: there are more possible positions than atoms in the universe, so you can't brute-force it. You have to have something like intuition.

You don't need to know the rules. Kohs films it like a sports documentary, cutting between the DeepMind engineers, the commentators and a stunned Korean public. The big moment is Move 37 in game two, a move so strange the experts thought it was a mistake, until it won. Then, in game four, Lee answers with a move of his own.

Why I love it: it's the moment the AI story we're all living through actually started, and nearly ten years on it lands harder than when it came out. But what stays with you is Lee Sedol: how one person carries a whole country's hopes into the room, and what it costs him.

New and worth it

🏆 Oscar & BAFTA winner 2026

Mr Nobody Against Putin

2025 · 90 min · IMDb 7.4 · RT 100%

When Russia invades Ukraine, the school videographer in a small industrial town is told to film the new patriotic lessons. So he does, and quietly keeps the footage. What you get is the war seen from inside a Russian classroom, filmed by someone who was never meant to be making a documentary. It won this year's Oscar and BAFTA because there's simply nothing else like it.

🏆 Oscar nominee · Sundance Directing Award

The Perfect Neighbor

2025 · 97 min · IMDb 7.1 · RT 98% · Netflix

A row between neighbours in Florida over kids playing on a patch of grass, told almost entirely through police bodycam footage. You watch the same calls come in again and again, and you know where it's heading long before anyone on screen does. No narrator and no talking heads, which is exactly why it's so hard to shake off.

🏆 Sundance Directing Award

2000 Meters to Andriivka

2025 · 108 min · IMDb 8.4 · RT 94% · Free on YouTube in the US

From Mstyslav Chernov, who won the Oscar for 20 Days in Mariupol. A Ukrainian platoon has to cross one mile of fortified forest to take back a village, and the film goes every metre of it with them. It's the closest most of us will ever get to understanding what this war actually asks of the people fighting it.

New this year

Louis Theroux: Inside the Manosphere

2026 · 92 min · RT 76% · Netflix

Louis does what Louis does best: turns up, asks polite questions and lets people talk themselves into a corner. This time it's the online world of ultra-masculine influencers. Uncomfortable and funny in equal measure, and worth watching if you've got teenagers.
